📖 Class 10 Notes

Light: Reflection & Refraction

  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u.
  • Mirror magnification: m = −v/u = h′/h.
  • Refractive index = speed in vacuum ÷ speed in medium.
  • Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v − 1/u; power: P = 1/f (f in metres).

Human Eye & Colourful World

  • Retina is the light-sensitive screen of the eye.
  • Accommodation: eye lens changes focal length to focus objects.
  • Myopia: distant objects unclear; corrected using a concave lens.
  • Hypermetropia: nearby objects unclear; corrected using a convex lens.
  • Dispersion is splitting of white light into its constituent colours.

Electricity

  • Current: I = Q/t; potential difference: V = W/Q.
  • Ohm's law: V = IR.
  • Series: R = R₁ + R₂ + …
  • Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
  • Power: P = VI = I²R = V²/R; energy = Pt.

Magnetic Effects of Current

  • A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field.
  • Right-hand thumb rule gives the field direction around a straight conductor.
  • Electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.
  • Fuse protects a circuit against excessive current.
  • Practise magnetic field-line and domestic-circuit diagrams.

Chemical Reactions & Equations

  • A balanced equation has equal numbers of each type of atom on both sides.
  • Combination: two or more reactants form one product.
  • Decomposition: one compound breaks into simpler substances.
  • Displacement: a more reactive element displaces a less reactive one.
  • Oxidation involves gain of oxygen or loss of hydrogen; reduction is the reverse.

Acids, Bases & Salts

  • Acids produce H⁺ ions in aqueous solution; bases produce OH⁻ ions.
  • Acid + base → salt + water (neutralisation).
  • pH below 7: acidic; 7: neutral; above 7: basic.
  • Always add acid to water slowly, not water to concentrated acid.
  • Revise formulae and uses of baking soda, washing soda and plaster of Paris.

Metals & Non-metals

  • Metals are generally malleable, ductile and good conductors.
  • Reactivity series arranges metals in decreasing order of reactivity.
  • More reactive metals can displace less reactive metals from salt solutions.
  • Ionic compounds usually have high melting points and conduct when molten or dissolved.
  • Corrosion can be reduced by painting, oiling, galvanising or alloying.

Carbon & Its Compounds

  • Carbon forms covalent bonds and shows catenation.
  • Saturated hydrocarbons have single bonds; unsaturated ones have double or triple bonds.
  • Successive members of a homologous series differ by –CH₂–.
  • Ethanol contains –OH; ethanoic acid contains –COOH.
  • Soap forms micelles; detergents work in hard water too.

Life Processes

  • Nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion are essential life processes.
  • Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide and water to form glucose and oxygen in sunlight with chlorophyll.
  • Human digestion: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.
  • Alveoli provide a large surface area for gas exchange in lungs.
  • Nephrons are the filtering units of kidneys.

Control & Coordination

  • Neurons carry electrical impulses; a synapse is the junction between neurons.
  • Brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system.
  • Reflex actions are quick, automatic responses.
  • Hormones are chemical messengers released by endocrine glands.
  • Plants respond through growth movements and chemical coordination.

How Do Organisms Reproduce?

  • Asexual reproduction involves one parent and no fusion of gametes.
  • Examples: fission, budding, fragmentation, regeneration and spore formation.
  • Sexual reproduction involves formation and fusion of male and female gametes.
  • Pollination is transfer of pollen to the stigma.
  • Fertilisation forms a zygote.

Heredity

  • Genes are units of inheritance made of DNA.
  • Dominant traits express in a heterozygous condition; recessive traits need two recessive alleles.
  • Acquired traits generally are not inherited.
  • Food chains show transfer of energy between organisms.
  • About 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level.
